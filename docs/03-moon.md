# Moon renderer reference

The independent `globe` crate renders an illustrative Moon; scientific terrain visibility is computed in `core/` and displayed by `app/`.

- **Coordinates:** latitude is north-positive and longitude east-positive. Unit directions are `(cos(lat) sin(lon), sin(lat), cos(lat) cos(lon))`: north is +y and longitude zero is +z. See [`math.rs`](../globe/src/math.rs).
- **Projection:** matrices use column-major storage. `View` supplies model rotation, zoom-dependent camera distance and an additive NDC pan shared by CPU overlays, picking and GPU projection. The fragment shader subtracts that lens shift when reconstructing sphere rays. See [`moon.vert`](../globe/src/moon.vert), [`moon.frag`](../globe/src/moon.frag) and [`deep.rs`](../globe/src/deep.rs).
- **Photometry:** the shader uses common LROC WAC 566 nm Hapke parameters with relative RGB texture modulation, not per-pixel spectral calibration. Photometry uses parallel observer rays, so the close zoom camera cannot create a localized opposition hotspot. Sun and Earth directions remain Moon-fixed from the ephemeris. Only reflected Earth light illuminates the solar night side, with an optional explicit exposure boost. This does not change terrain visibility results.
- **Grid:** the decorative zoom grid appears at 3x and above when zoom changes, holds for one second, then fades out over half a second. It does not snap clicks.
- **Color:** albedo is decoded from sRGB for linear-light shading and encoded for display; relief-derived object-space normals are decoded and renormalized in the shader.
- **GL lifetime:** renderer resources belong to the eframe/glow context. Painting uses an egui GL callback; explicit destruction releases GPU resources. See [`renderer.rs`](../globe/src/renderer.rs) and [`view.rs`](../globe/src/view.rs).

## Quality and anti-aliasing

The Lighting options contain one **Quality: Fast / Realistic** choice. Fast uses satellite contrast with a cheap lunar-Lambert response and no normal-map sampling, terrain marching or visibility mask. Height textures are requested only after choosing Realistic; base color/normal pairs remain transactionally loaded for mode/tier switching. Realistic retains Hapke, normals and DEM shadows without adding recorded photographic shadows. Earthshine remains separate in both modes. Mobile/2k devices default to Fast; desktop browsers default to the 4k tier and Realistic (native desktops retain their existing tier). The selection is remembered in eframe storage when available, otherwise for the lifetime of the view.

Separate compile-time shader variants remove terrain and Hapke work from Fast. Both rasterize a tight projected-disc quad with a two-pixel coverage margin, discard off-disc fragments, and blend analytic pixel-width limb coverage. Texture coordinates use seam-safe explicit derivatives; color/normal textures have trilinear mipmaps with up to 2x anisotropy when supported. Satellite arrays use trilinear mipmaps without anisotropy to keep Fast inexpensive. Packed heights and page tables remain unmipmapped data. Shadow masks retain bilinear upsampling and are cached by camera/Sun/terrain state. Uniform locations and sampler assignments are initialized once per program; only active textures are bound. Realistic stops requesting photographic tiles after its complete surface is ready. Loading, animation and the existing anti-aliased egui overlays retain event-driven repaints. No extra supersampling or post-process pass is needed; native MSAA remains enabled as before.

Loading remains transactional: global color/normal, both height maps and polar normals are staged together, with one GPU upload per frame. The previous complete surface remains visible during tier changes; initial Realistic loading uses the Fast overview until the complete set and visibility mask are ready. Satellite imagery stays on the overview until all currently requested tiles are resident, rather than exposing partially downloaded hemispheres. Failed replacements retain the existing complete surface.

## Zoom detail

The camera supports 2,048x. Above 16x, bounded view windows supplement, rather than replace, the overview and its picking geometry. No additional image or DEM requests occur at startup. The Fast-only browser preview reuses its cached color as an inert normal sampler, avoiding the unused 160 KiB preview normal transfer; full surface normal tiers remain unchanged.

Fast requests a 4x4 NAC photographic tile window only within NASA Trek's available regional mosaics or the polar NAC mosaics above 86 degrees. The EQ catalogue bounds and capability-limited levels are in `globe/src/detail/regions.rs`; the global WAC streamer remains the fallback elsewhere. Providers offer EQ levels through 14 and polar levels through 11; requested levels adapt to the viewport footprint. At a typical 1,024x view they are EQ 10 (20.82 m/texel) and polar 9 (16.72 m/texel), one level finer at 2,048x. These are recorded photographs, not current shadow measurements. Four downloads run concurrently; all sixteen tiles must decode successfully, with opaque coverage, before one complete mipmapped window is published. A missing tile retains the previous complete window/overview. Rounded tile-window centering and a central-half reuse margin prevent small camera reversals from repeatedly replacing overlapping windows.

The WAC atlas also publishes transactionally: its previous complete page table and referenced texture layers remain unchanged while a new visible set loads. Only a complete replacement set can become visible; failed downloads retain the published set instead of flashing back to overview albedo. Selection uses camera/zoom hysteresis, and an active mosaic remains enabled down to 1.7x rather than toggling at 2x. Both WAC selection and adjacent NAC detail levels tolerate a 0.14 log-zoom change before replacement. The lazy atlas reserves 160 layers for two disjoint 80-tile sets (53.33 MiB including mipmaps, formerly 32 MiB); its network queue and per-frame upload budget are unchanged. No extra startup downloads are introduced.

Realistic instead requests one 512-square F32 LOLA DEM window and derives object-space normals from measured three-dimensional surface differences. It uses the global 256ppd service (118.45 m native spacing), or the 5 m / 30 m polar services within their coverage. Sampling never exceeds source resolution; quantized windows include a view margin and are reused while unchanged. The decoder rejects invalid dimensions, nonfinite elevations, NoData and implausible radius-valued samples. The same decoded heights also drive fine terrain shadows. Neither NAC detail photographs nor WAC/polar photographic contrast are sampled in Realistic: baked shadows cannot fight the selected date's moving Sun. Relief still does not displace the illustrative sphere's silhouette.

Window uploads are atomic: a Realistic normal texture and its matching height texture must both upload successfully before replacing the old pair and invalidating its shadow mask. Failed requests, invalid payloads or upload errors retain the old pair. Normal/photo textures are mipmapped, trilinearly filtered and optionally 2x anisotropic; packed heights use linear base-level filtering only. A smooth spatial border joins the previous overview. Only the active quality's window is sampled, and Fast never requests a DEM. GPU storage is bounded to one active and one transient replacement window (approximately 5.33 MiB for a photographic window, 2.33 MiB for normals plus heights, including normal mipmaps). No additional network requests or startup downloads are needed for fine shadows. Changing the viewpoint while a request is in flight does not launch an unbounded queue. Scientific custom-point horizons, markers, exact-point picking and the timeline are independent of this visual detail.

## Terrain shadows

Global 2k/4k and south-polar 1k/2k heights use the same source DEMs and projections as the normals. Heights are linear RGB8: RG stores a 16-bit value (`value * 0.5 - 10000` metres), B marks validity. `heights.manifest.json` records scale, offset, dimensions, spacing, hashes and download sizes. The 8k visual tier uses 4k heights; no higher-resolution DEM is implied.

The shader marches along the selected date's Moon-fixed Sun direction over a sphere of radius 1,737,400 m. Inside an active detail window, RG stores half-metre heights with a -15,000 m offset, retaining the same validated F32 source as its normals. Heights feather continuously into the global/polar height field over the window's outer six percent. Up to 768 near samples, one detail pixel apart starting at half a pixel, resolve the window before the existing 32/48 geometrically increasing distant samples continue out to 600 km; sixteen extra samples refine the strongest distant candidate. Fine origins use a 0.5 m numerical bias; overview-only origins retain 3 m. Curvature and the core solver's 0.534° solar disk give smooth penumbras. Height-field visibility replaces the reference-sphere solar cutoff, allowing elevated terrain beyond the terminator without lighting deep-night normal-map facets.

A reduced-resolution visibility mask (up to 256² low tier / 512² high tier) is linearly upsampled and cached until camera, Sun, terrain tier, polar normals or the complete detail pair change. Fine marching therefore costs a mask rebuild, not a per-frame full-resolution march. The CPU reference tests compare the same near/far marching geometry and solar disk with `lunar_core::horizon_from_terrain` on identical quantized, interpolated fine/coarse synthetic DEMs at equatorial and polar points, including two dated ephemeris directions, a resolved crater rim and a distant ridge. This is still illustrative terrain lighting, not the scientific horizon solver: subpixel ridges and narrow distant features between growing samples can be missed, mask edges are softened, relief does not alter the globe silhouette, and source-resolution transitions are feathered rather than eliminated. Global detail is limited to 118.45 m native data; polar export spacing depends on the loaded window.
